# Watch-time rules (style-independent)

Apply to every script in `queue/`, whatever style is active. Based on our own reels and a frame-by-frame analysis (2026-11-13) of 11 narrator reference reels: bombilla (culturargentiina), football hen, fox photography, path to pope, cup noodles, football shirts, lobizón legend, Qatar final, horno de barro, "¿Qué hacés?" POV story, 24 h counter. Reference links: project memory `reference-reels`.

**Rules that follow (most important first):**
1. **The topic must be about the viewer's own everyday life** – "tu perro", "tu celular", "tu beagle", "si te pusieras a trabajar", "si te pasaron las siete". Abstract, technical or "¿sabías esto de…?" topics flop (microwave, headlights, cinema coughing) even with the same look. For us: *tu mate, tu yerba, tu termo, tu abuela, tu ronda, vos*.
2. **Reframe something everybody does or sees** – "no es mala educación: es su trabajo", "lo hace a propósito". Take a familiar habit and reveal the hidden reason.
3. **Fact + emotional twist in one sentence** – "te ve borroso… y aún así no te confunde con nadie". The second half hits a feeling (love, pride, disgust, laughter).
4. **Strong feelings beat information:** affection (pets, abuela), disgust ("más sucio que…"), humour/provocation ("si te pusieras a trabajar"), national pride (Maradona), identity.
5. **Identity tests get shared:** "Si te pasaron las siete, sos argentino de verdad" → for us "Si hacés esto con el mate, sos argentino de verdad".
6. **Big debates everyone has an opinion on** ("huevo o gallina", Maradona vs goles, mate argentino o uruguayo, dulce o amargo) → many comments; promise an answer "que no le va a gustar a nadie".
7. **The hook sentence is also the first line of the post caption**, written as a full statement (+1–2 emojis), not a title.
8. **Series + "¿qué … sigue?"** at the end (prexa.ar "¿Qué raza sigue?") turns viewers into commenters and returning followers. A keyword comment ("Comentá MATE…") also works.

**New rules from this batch:**
9. **"Team A o Team B" in the hook** (651k, and the same channel's "team melón o durazno" 5k): a two-sided choice with a wink makes everybody comment their side. For us: "¿Team dulce o team amargo?", "¿Team calabaza o team acero?".
10. **Number chain → surprising transformation** (bosta → 15 toneladas → luz para cien casas): start with a small everyday number, multiply it, end on an unexpected result. For us: "Un argentino toma X mates por día → en un año → en una vida…".
11. **Origin of an everyday word** (ojalá 406k vs "¿sabías que…?" 188): every viewer uses the word daily. For us: "mate", "bombilla", "cebar", "che", "yerba".
12. **"Si ves esto… NO lo hagas"** – a warning about something in your own home that goes against instinct.
13. **Local rivalry and identity** (bostero, Maradona, "sos argentino de verdad") and a **tag-a-friend ending** ("Etiquetá a tu amigo más … que …") – shares spread the reel.
14. **Avoid:** "¿Sabías que…?" trivia openers, do-this-test challenges, niche/technical how-tos, "Parte 2" without a strong hook of its own, duplicated news. They flop on every channel.

## 1. The hook (0–2 s) decides everything
- **One strong image + one claim, from frame 0.** Every reference opens on a single, instantly readable picture that already carries the headline (frame 0 = thumbnail): crossed-out bombilla "SIN BOMBILLA", a hen in a football shirt, the finished prize photo, the Pope next to the flag, a cup labelled "FIDEOS", a wall of 30 shirts, a scoreboard at minute 6'.
- **Hook types that worked:**
  1. *Surprising fact as a contradiction* – "Antes de la bombilla, el colador eran los dientes."
  2. *"¿Por qué…?" question with a strange detail* – "¿Por qué el presidente es padrino del séptimo hijo varón?"
  3. *Result first* – show the finished thing (prize photo, finished oven), then "así se hace".
  4. *Topical hook* – "Ahora que el Papa…", "La final de Qatar…".
  5. *"Qué hacés si…" dilemma in 2nd person* – puts the viewer inside the story.
- Spoken hook ≤ 10 words; the headline appears by 0.4 s; no greeting, no channel intro.
- The hook picture stays 3–6 s (it is not cut away early): the first beats are explained **on** that same picture with small overlays (X marks, labels, numbers).

## 2. Open loop by second 5–10
- Promise something that only comes later: hidden "?" cards to be revealed, "y la tercera es la más rara", a running clock (6' → 26' → 45' …), "uno por uno", a ladder of steps.
- **Visual counters keep people watching:** a clock, a score, a step number, an hour counter, a growing build. Use one when the topic allows (`number`, `timeline`, `list` visuals, numbered steps).

## 3. Body: one idea per picture, 2–4 s each
- New visual change every 2–4 s (a new shot, a reveal, a label, a zoom); a full scene change every 4–8 s.
- Visual grammar that repeats in almost all references: numbered cards/pins, red X for the wrong way, check for the right way, label pills pointing at parts, big numbers on a solid colour, a comparison side by side.
- **Escalation:** order points from known → surprising → most surprising. The strongest fact goes last before the payoff.
- One recurring **hero** (a mascot, the same object, the same character or set) gives continuity: the bombilla reel keeps one gourd for 18 s, the oven reel keeps one diorama for 118 s.

## 4. Re-hooks every 8–12 s
- A short pattern break: "Pero esperá…", a sudden close-up, a colour change, a sound hit, a question to "vos". Mark with `impact: true`.

## 5. Payoff (last 20 %)
- Deliver the promise with the strongest picture, then **land on an emotion or a joke** (pope reel ends on a laughing mascot "¡JAJA!", the oven on everyone eating, the cebador on "compañía").

## 6. Ending that creates comments and replays
- **Comment bait in the last 3 s:** a choice with 2–4 short options shown as buttons/pills ("¿Vos cuál usás?", "Comentá 1 o 2", "¿Cuál querés en la parte 2?"). The comments under the references show it works: people answer the options and suggest the next topic.
- **Series hook:** "Parte 1", "¿qué leyenda querés en la parte 2?" → followers come back.
- **Loop:** the last frame matches the first (hen → hen, shirts → shirts, gourd → gourd), so the replay is seamless and counts as extra watch time.
- "Seguinos para más" in the last sentence.

## 7. Captions, text, sound
- Small karaoke captions at the top, one highlighted word, readable on any background (box).
- On-screen headlines max 3 words; numbers big.
- The narrator never stops for more than ~0.6 s; music low under the voice; a soft hit on every reveal.
- Post caption repeats the hook, adds one value line and ends with the same question as the video (+ 5–8 hashtags). Credits for images/music when used.

## 8. Our format limits
- **Length: 35–45 s** (references run 58–118 s; we stay shorter for completion rate). With Charon: 12–14 segments (~100–110 words).
- Rioplatense, voseo, only Spanish on screen. 2–3 sensory/human moments per reel. No invented facts, no health claims.
- Vary `emo`, never the same three times in a row.

## Checklist for every script
0. Is the topic about the viewer's own everyday life (tu mate, tu abuela, vos), with a reframe or an emotional twist? If not, rewrite the angle.
1. Hook: one picture + one claim, frame 0 has the headline? Which hook type?
2. Open loop or visual counter by 5–10 s?
3. Points ordered by surprise, one idea per segment?
4. Re-hook every 8–12 s?
5. Payoff lands on emotion or a joke?
5b. Humour: reels may be extremely absurd (user, 2026-11-23). Wild exaggerations and impossible escalations are welcome in every style, as long as the situation stays relatable at its core. At least one silly moment per reel (a sentence with emo "funny": slapstick, an exaggerated reaction, a cheeky comparison, self-irony). The reels may be goofy; in 3D every funny sentence becomes a gag on screen (trip, spit-take, goofy face, happy dance) with the others laughing until they cry, at most three per reel.
6. Ending: choice with options (or "parte 2" question) + "Seguinos para más", last picture echoes the first?

## 9. First numbers of our own reels (2026-11-16, 10 reels, public counts)
- Every reel got 1,200-1,800 views (the test audience of a new account); none broke out yet. Like rate 5 % on the first two days, 2-3 % on the third.
- Comments came almost only on the two debates ("dulce o amargo" 4, "argentino o uruguayo" 3; 9 in total). Consequences for scripts:
  1. Prefer debate / team / identity topics; at least one of the three reels of a day is a debate.
  2. Ask the comment question early: the styles show "¿VOS QUÉ DECÍS?" with the poll options in the second sentence. Every script that can carry a choice gets a `poll`.
  3. At most three text-only boards per reel; a sentence that can be shown is shown (scene, object, compare, map).
- 2D vs 3D: no visible difference in two pairs (both 3D reels were the old one-camera look). Compare again with the numbers of `insights/REPORT.md` (written by `scripts/insights.js` after every post slot; watch time needs the permission instagram_manage_insights).

## 10. Breakout on our own channel (2026-11-17)
"El primer mate es el peor de la ronda… y el cebador se lo toma a propósito" (2D): 7,892 views, 20.7 s average watch, 23 shares, 17 saves, 8 comments; all other reels 1,200–1,800 views.
What it has that the others do not: an everyday ritual everyone knows + a hidden reason ("a propósito") that turns a habit into an insider secret + a clear villain/hero (the cebador) + a 3-option poll about the viewer's own habit. Shares come from "send this to the one who always takes the first one".
Use for new scripts: start with a habit the viewer does every day, reveal the secret reason in the first 3 s, name the person in the ronda it is about, end with "¿vos qué hacés?" and three options.
2D visual loop after it: one text at a time (no headline over boards), reactions in the place of the story instead of plain colour, the early question high and without the options line, groups of four in one row.

**What they all share (new rules, they add to sections 1–4):**
1. **Hard cuts are not the pace; change is.** Only 6–22 hard cuts per minute (we cut every ~1.5 s). The picture never stands still: a counter counts up, blur grows, letters assemble, a pile grows, the camera follows a moving person. Rule: *something visibly changes at least every second*, a new picture every 2–5 s.
2. **Frame 1 is the thing in action, never a title.** A growing pile, an eye, a man already riding the cart. No logo, no text card, no establishing shot.
3. **The hook sentence names a number or a habit of the viewer and finishes in under 4 s** ("Una vaca caga cincuenta kilos por día", "¿Sabías que cada vez que decís ojalá…" (the habit is the hook, not the trivia), "¿Qué pasaría si te pusieras a trabajar?").
4. **A label points at the viewer**: "TU SITIO" with an arrow at the empty sofa. For us: "TU MATE", "VOS", "TU ABUELA" with an arrow, in the first 5 s.
5. **First re-hook at 5–8 s, then every 8–12 s, each with a different phrase**: "Y no solo eso:", "Y no es por tu cara:", "…siguen en tu casa:", a CRACK! or crash. Our queue used "Pero esperá" in every script and only once at ~18 s; now there are 2–3 re-hooks per script with varied openers ("Y no solo eso:", "Ojo,", "Y lo más loco…", "Y acá está el secreto:", "Y lo peor:").
6. **Escalation**: every step is bigger than the last (17 → 36 kg → 15 toneladas → luz para cien casas; plank → planks → deck → truss → pillars; sofa → dog → abuela crying).
7. **Payoff comes back to the viewer's home** ("siguen en tu casa: almohada, azúcar, aceite") and the end is a comment or share prompt ("Dime qué otra curiosidad…", "Mandale este video a tu amigo más bostero").
8. **Captions**: one word (wemakebuild, agrimporta) or the growing sentence with the current word highlighted (ojalá, perro); key words in yellow.

These checks run automatically: `node scripts/lint.js` (all queue scripts, never blocks; daily build logs it). After this pass the queue went from 83 to 5 findings.

## 12. wemakebuild vs our build3d: people, objects and how they interact
| | wemakebuild | our build3d before |
|---|---|---|
| Scale | the object is human-sized: a man sits in the cart, carries the box, walks on the plank | a small mate on a table, people stand behind it |
| Interaction | people ride, push, carry, hammer, fall into and fly out of the object | people point, drink, pour next to the object |
| Failure gag | every version fails on the person: CRACK!, falls in the river, flies into a tree, crime-scene tape | failures are a red stamp on the object |
| Recurring hero | one named guy (Kevin) + a friend who pushes, presents or laughs | changing people per shot |
| Camera | few hard cuts; follow / chase camera on the moving person, static diorama while the object evolves | a cut every 1.5 s with a new camera |
| World | one place, cut open like a diorama (soil cross-section under the river) | sunny lawn, cut block |
| Progress | the object evolves in steps, each step a new problem (rain, weight, width) | stages of the gourd |
| Lens | phone-like: ~1x for the diorama, **0.5x ultra-wide** very close to the person in chase and face shots (big head and hands in front, the world bends away behind) | fixed 42° (≈ 2x tele): flat, far away |
| Figures in wide shots | small (~1/8 of the frame height); close-ups for faces | medium |
| Text | one white word in the middle, technical names and gags as big yellow outlined labels ("CRACK!", "TRUSSES", "17 COMPLAINTS") | same |
| Ending | callback: the first rider ends as the ride's employee; Kevin ends as the pilot | — |

Applied in build3d: a lens per camera (engine LENS table: chase 104°, low 100°, face 96°, follow 92°, wide shots 56–64°; the camera moves closer so the subject keeps its size), new acts carry / ride / slide / launch, a chase camera, the translator cuts about every 2.5 s instead of 1.5 s and turns every mistake into a fall gag; example samples/build3d_tito.js. Also: a named hero who physically handles a big object (carries, sits in, pushes it), a friend who reacts, failure gags that hit the person (fall, fly, crack), follow cameras instead of cut-cut-cut, the object evolving in place.
