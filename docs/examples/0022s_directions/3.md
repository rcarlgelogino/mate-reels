Sentence: "Al otro día cebás el primero, medio dormido, y sale espuma." About 3.5 seconds.

What the viewer must understand without sound: it is the next morning, earlier, you have just got up and are half asleep, you prepare your first mate as every day without paying attention, and when you pour the hot water the mate foams over like a washing machine. The time jump must be clear at a glance: the clock shows 7:30 instead of 8:00, the light is paler and cooler, the window shows an early sky, and you look freshly out of bed.

Link to the shot before: yesterday the mother put the mate upside down in the dish rack after one quick rinse. This morning you take it from the dish rack, still half asleep, fill it with yerba and pour. We show the first second of you taking it from the rack, so the viewer connects it with the mother's scrubbing.

The set in this shot. The same kitchen, the same camera height (eyes of an adult), this time centred a little more to the right, on the counter between the sink and the termo. The back wall: cream tiles, light green paint. The window on the left: the curtains hang still, there is no wind yet; the sky outside is pale blue with a soft pink band near the horizon, the lemon tree is in shadow, its lemons look pale. The light comes softly from the window, no hard sun patch yet; the kitchen is a bit grey-blue, and the ceiling lamp (a simple white bowl lamp hanging from the top of the frame on a short cord) is switched on and gives a warm yellow circle of light on the counter. The clock on the right wall shows 7:30, its red second hand ticking. The sink is empty and dry: no foam, no yerba, no pot. The dish rack holds only the mate upside down, the bombilla lying next to it, and one cup. The detergent bottle still stands dented on the sink edge, label to the camera. On the counter: the termo, the open yerba pack, and a plate with two medialunas from yesterday under a paper napkin. On the fridge at the right edge we still see the child's drawing and the magnets. The hall door on the right is half open, the hall behind it dark.

You. You stand behind the counter in the middle of the frame (x 0.5), facing the camera, shoulders hanging, slightly bent forward. You are about 25, slim, dark brown hair that sticks up on one side and is flat on the other, a red crease from the pillow on the left cheek. You wear a grey hoodie with the hood half on, half off, and blue pyjama trousers with small white clouds (only the top visible above the counter). You are barefoot (not visible).

Your movements.
At 0.0 your right hand reaches into the dish rack without looking and takes the mate, turns it the right way up; your left hand takes the bombilla. Your eyes are almost closed, two thin lines, your mouth hangs open in a long yawn, your head tilts to the side. Your body sways slowly left and right like a tree in the wind, about one sway per two seconds.
At 0.15 you stick the bombilla into the mate, then tip yerba from the pack into it with a shaky hand (some yerba falls next to it on the counter), without looking, the yawn ending in a small smack of the lips.
At 0.3 (on "cebás") you take the termo in your right hand and the mate in your left, at chest height. You tilt the termo and a thin stream of steaming water pours into the mate beside the bombilla. Your eyes are still closed, a small content smile on your face, you hum.
At 0.55 (on "medio dormido") your head sinks forward a little as if you were falling asleep standing, then jerks back up a centimetre.
At 0.6 (on "sale espuma") the foam comes. Your eyes snap wide open, your eyebrows jump up to the hairline, your mouth turns into a small round o, your head jerks back, your chin pulls in. You stop pouring at once, the termo frozen in the air, a last drop falling from its spout. Your left hand with the mate stays where it is, you stare at it from as far away as your arm allows. The foam runs over your fingers; you spread the fingers a little, disgusted, but you do not let go.

Objects and how they end. The mate is in your left hand, full of yerba and water, the foam spilling over its rim, running down the gourd, over your fingers, and dripping onto the counter into a small white puddle that keeps growing. The termo is in your right hand, held up. Some dry yerba lies on the counter. The detergent bottle on the sink edge is in the frame behind you, so a viewer who looks closely can connect the foam with it.

Camera. Medium shot, you from the hips up, the counter, the termo, the yerba and the mate in the frame, the clock visible at the top right. Still until 0.55. When the foam starts, a quick soft push-in (about 20 percent in half a second) toward the mate and your face, ending with your face in the upper third and the foaming mate in the lower third; never cut the mate or your eyes. Focus on you; the window slightly soft.

Timing on the words: "Al otro día" = the clock at 7:30 and the pale light are visible from the first frame, you take the mate from the rack. "cebás el primero" = the water pours. "medio dormido" = your head nods. "y sale espuma" = the foam rises and your face jumps awake.

Effects and sounds. Steam rises from the termo spout and the mate in thin curls. At 0.55 white foam rises out of the mate like a soufflé, slowly at first, then faster, building a dome higher than the mate, then spilling over. At 0.65 two small bubbles float up from the dome to your face and pop near your nose. Sounds: soft morning birds outside, the water pouring, your humming, then a growing bubbling noise like a washing machine starting, a wet drip on the counter.

The gag: the sleepy morning ritual turns into a washing machine in your hand; your face goes from asleep to wide awake in one frame.

Continuity to the next shot: the foam stays on the rim, the termo goes back on the counter, the mate stays in your hand; you are still in the hoodie with the pillow mark.
