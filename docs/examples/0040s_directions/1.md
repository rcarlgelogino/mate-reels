Sentence: "En una ronda de mate, decir gracias es lo peor que podés hacer." About 3.8 seconds. Hook.

What the viewer must understand without sound: a sunny family patio, a ronda of mate around a round table, everyone relaxed; the mate goes to the young man in the light blue shirt, he drinks, gives it back with a polite nod, and in that very moment the cebador lifts one eyebrow and the whole table freezes for a beat. Something just went wrong, and the viewer wants to know what. Context (family ronda), curiosity (what did he do?) and the snapback (polite = wrong) must all be in these four seconds.

Why this picture: a ronda is a circle, and a circle is what 3D does best. We open high and wide, looking down at the whole circle like a drone, so the viewer sees all five people and the patio in one look, then the camera sinks and swings around to the young man while the mate travels to him. The movement of the camera follows the movement of the mate.

The set in this shot. A family patio in the afternoon. Under the table a round floor of terracotta bricks, about eight metres across, laid in rows of warm orange, slightly different tones. Around it short green grass. At the back a low white wall with a terracotta cap, dark green bushes in front of it; on the left behind the table a lemon tree with yellow lemons; at the back right a brick parrilla with glowing embers, five chorizos on the grill and white smoke curling up into the sky. On the left a white side wall with a brown wooden door. Over the patio a string of light bulbs in yellow, pink and light blue hangs in a soft curve from left to right, the bulbs glowing faintly in the daylight. In the centre a round wooden table with a white tablecloth; on it a red-and-white yerba pack lying on its side and a white plate with two medialunas. Around the table four low wooden chairs with straw seats, one for each seated person, all turned towards the centre. The suegro, his girlfriend's father and the cebador (about 58): grey-brown short hair under a black boina, a thick moustache, a dark red polo shirt, grey trousers; he does not sit, he stands at the back of the table with the green termo under his left arm and the mate in his right hand.

Movements, beat by beat.
At 0.0 the suegro stands at the back right of the table between the abuela and vos, the mate in his right hand held out towards vos, the termo in his left arm. His face is friendly, eyes half closed in the calm of an old cebador.
At 0.1 vos turns his head to the suegro and reaches out his left hand to the side; the mate travels from the suegro's hand to his on a small arc (the hand-over takes about a third of a second).
At 0.3 vos brings the mate to his mouth and drinks: two quick sips, eyes closed, cheeks pulled in. Everyone else watches him: the abuela turns her head to him, the novia smiles at him with her chin on her hand, the cuñado raises his chin.
At 0.62 vos lowers the mate, smiles wide, nods once with his whole upper body and holds the mate out to the suegro: the mate travels back. This is the moment he says "gracias".
At 0.75 the suegro takes it and stops. One eyebrow goes up (sly face), his head tilts. The abuela's eyes go to the cuñado, the cuñado's eyes go to the novia: a silent look around the table.
At 0.9 the dog lifts its head and looks at vos too.

Camera. Starts high and wide in front of the table, slightly to the right (about 20 degrees around), 4.6 metres away, 3.2 metres up, looking down at the centre of the table: the whole circle and most of the patio are in the picture. During the shot it sinks to 2.2 metres and moves closer to 3.4 metres while swinging to about 40 degrees, so that at the end vos and the suegro fill the middle of the frame and the faces of all five are readable. The movement is smooth and slow, never a jump. Nobody's head is cut.
