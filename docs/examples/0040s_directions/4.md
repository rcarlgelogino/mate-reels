Sentence: "Y desde ese momento, el mate te esquiva." About 3.6 seconds.

What the viewer must understand without sound: the suegro walks around the table serving everyone one after the other, and when he comes to vos he simply walks past him. Vos even raises his hand a little, and the suegro does not stop. The mate literally goes around him.

Why this picture: the circle again, now in motion. The camera turns around the whole table together with the suegro, so the viewer feels the round being completed without vos.

The set: the whole patio as in the hook, seen from 2.4 metres height; as the camera circles, the background changes: the parrilla with smoke, the white back wall and bushes, the lemon tree, the side wall with the door, the sleeping dog in the front.

Movements, beat by beat.
At 0.0 the suegro stands behind the novia (back left), pours hot water from the termo into the mate (the termo tilts, a thin stream), then hands her the mate. She drinks and gives it back.
At 0.3 he walks around the table to the cuñado (front left) and does the same. The cuñado takes it with one hand without uncrossing his legs, drinks, gives it back.
At 0.55 the suegro walks on to the front right, right past vos. Vos lifts his left hand halfway, opens his mouth to say something; his face shows hope. The suegro does not even look at him; he walks on, humming, eyes on the mate.
At 0.7 he stops behind the abuela (back right), pours, hands her the mate. Vos slowly lowers his hand, his face falls (sad), shoulders sink.
At 0.9 the abuela drinks with relish; vos looks at her mate.

Camera. An orbit around the table: it starts behind-left (about 300 degrees), 3.4 metres from the centre, 2.4 metres high, and turns smoothly about 120 degrees during the shot, always looking at the centre of the table at 0.7 metres height. The suegro stays roughly in the middle of the frame because the camera turns with him.

Timing: "Y desde ese momento" = serving the novia. "el mate" = serving the cuñado. "te esquiva" = he walks past vos, the hand goes up and down.

Sounds: footsteps on the bricks, the pour, a small hum of the suegro, a sad "wah" when the hand goes down.

The gag: the hand that goes up full of hope and is ignored.

Continuity: the round goes on; in the next shot time passes.
