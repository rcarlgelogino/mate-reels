## The lessons for our reels (from code-built reels only)
1. **The story engine beats render quality.** aagekyahoga.stories (Claude, characters in code, simple flat 2D): 7.8M views in one week, because every episode ends on a dilemma for the viewer ("write one word: KEEP or BURN") and the next episode follows the majority ("79 % said OPEN"). Their 9 steps per episode: series bible, script, voice, characters in code, every shot, sound, a check of every frame, post, vote.
2. **Frame 0 = the premise and the threat.** morrow.studio3d (Claude Code, three.js + Blender), 5.1M views in 46 hours: the threat on frame 0, an **If → But → So** chain, every build step on its word; a source for every number; every frame checked.
3. **Density.** morrow: 75 shots, 102 sound effects, 4,200 frames for about 60 s (a new picture about every 0.8 s). We use 8–12 shots for 30–40 s.
4. **Formats that work in code-built 3D:** "what if …" with a running counter and a countdown (morrow 2.7–12M); "how X becomes Y" build time-lapse from one almost fixed camera (wine barrel → hideout 10.8M, doghouse 6.4M, bamboo → house 5.6M, staircase → safe room 1.5M); counter escalation on one object (buildwithdarius: potato vs 1,000 knife cuts); cutaway / section view (morrow's barrel cut open: the hider inside, the soldiers outside = the viewer knows more than the characters); true-story history shorts (toonwright "Ricks Clips style", devychip "The Bridge That Fell For a Song"); isometric miniature dioramas with many small moving parts (slashmanuel port, thinxai island, omnyalab stadium).
5. **Series and votes.** aagekyahoga, i.asked.claude ("vote in the poll, episode 2 follows your vote"), toonwright ("your comments write the next episode").
6. **Light, not more objects, makes it look expensive.** hashhh.co (Claude): cool moonlight + warm campfire as the second light, sparks only near the fire, shallow depth of field, one location. clipcat: "cheap videos light everything the same; the eye goes to the brightest spot".
7. **Motion has weight** (clipcat on Claude animations): Claude takes the safe default for 1,000 decisions (neutral, flat, one steady speed); fix it with anticipation ("pull back, then jump"), ease, overshoot, and decide movement, light and sound before frame one.
8. **Camera by feeling** (dsqracademy, Claude 3D animator skill): push in = tension, over the shoulder = conversation, POV = be the hero, handheld = real, bullet time = the impossible moment. morrow's builds use an almost fixed camera so every step reads.
9. **Captions:** 2–3 words in the middle of the frame, the key word coloured (morrow); numbers sit on the objects as labels ("500 LB").
10. **Looks Claude builds well:** low-poly with strong two-colour light (hashhh), voxel / blocky (morrow, toonwright Roblox look), isometric diorama, painterly paper collage (framelab), flat 2D (aagekyahoga), toy-like three.js cartoons (cartoon-3d-short).

## What this means for mate reels
- Serial with a vote: end on a dilemma ("¿Le devolvés el mate o te lo tomás?"), comment one word, the next episode follows the majority.
- Build time-lapse in 3D: "cómo una calabaza se convierte en tu mate" (harvest, dry, cut, cure; a day counter), "del árbol de yerba al paquete".
- What-if with a counter: "¿Qué pasa si toda Argentina ceba a la vez?".
- Cutaway: inside the gourd (why it absorbs soap), inside the mouth when the mate burns.
- Our own data adds the family situation (suegra 69k): the same rules apply to it (threat on frame 0, If → But → So, a vote at the end).

## Round 1 + 2 (about 210 AI reels): what the successful ones do

### Formats that get millions of views (3D, made with Claude)
- **"What if …?" mass scenario** (morrow.studio3d: "what if every human farted at the same time", "what if all rubber disappeared for 5 seconds in Times Square" 2.7M, 3D builds 12.4M / 8.2M on YouTube): one absurd but instantly clear question, a real place (beach, Times Square), thousands of simple low-poly people, a big running counter on screen (1,015 -> 738,000,000 -> 8,200,000,000) and a countdown, a POV hand with a phone. Claude checks the facts first, then writes the script.
- **"How it's built" time-lapse** (morrow: "how a hole in your yard becomes a lazy river" 4.3M, "how a doghouse becomes a secret hideout" 6.4M; sabodominik: "how a pile of bamboo becomes a house", channels with 9M views/month; toonwright /build): one place, one fixed or slowly orbiting camera, the thing grows step by step, each step readable.
- **Counter escalation on one object** (buildwithdarius: "a potato vs 1,000 knife cuts"): macro shot on a board, counter 7 -> 36 -> 138 -> 796, the object changes visibly.
- **True-story history shorts** (toonwright "Ricks Clips style": "why he cut a gap in his own bridge", "the stairs that warned Egypt"; devychip example "The Bridge That Fell For a Song", soldiers marching on a bridge; the_unindexed stickman "what did ancient humans do when it rained"): one true event, simple toy 3D, camera among the characters.
- **Absurd world rule ("In this town …")** in Roblox/blocky look (toonwright, zaylento "your screen time controls the weather").
- **Episodic series with audience votes** (aagekyahoga.stories 7.8M in one week: series bible, script, voice, characters in code, every shot, sound, frame check, post, vote for the next part; i.asked.claude: poll decides episode 2; toonwright: comments write the next episode).
- **Relatable family moments with Pixar-like characters** (mama.clubnika "No one leaves Grandma's house without food and money"; clayrified clay film 5M): the same kind of topic as ours.
- **Fruit / object soap operas** (fruit.cries.too, frutimedia, mindset_relatable strawberry): anthropomorphic objects in human drama.

### Looks that read as "quality" in 3D
- **Low-poly / faceted stylisation with strong lighting** (hashhh.co campfire): cool moonlight + warm fire as the second light, motivated sparks only near the fire, shallow depth of field, one location for the whole reel, shots alternate close / over-the-shoulder / wide.
- **Isometric miniature diorama** (slashmanuel container port, thinxai floating island, karam.mahaan real-estate world, omnyalab stadium): tilt-shift, everything small and busy, many small moving parts (cranes, trucks, people).
- **Claymation / clay look** (clayrified 5M, uricurti styles): soft textures, warm light.
- **Realistic 3D explainer (Zack D Films style)** (trollix, karam.mahaan "zack-d-films" skill): cutaways / x-ray of what happens inside, cross-sections of ground or body.
- **Mascot on saturated solid colours** (devychip "Chip"): one character, background colour changes per shot, big text.

### Animation craft (from the craft reels)
- clipcat.app "If your Claude AI animation looks cheap": it is direction. Claude guesses 1,000 things and takes the safest choice each time (neutral, default, flat) = a boring video. Fixes: **movement** (no steady speed, nothing real moves like that; Disney's Illusion of Life, 1981: pull back, then jump = anticipation), **light** (cheap videos light everything the same; the eye goes to the brightest spot), **sound** (half of the experience), all **decided before frame one**.
- gogananimation: timing and spacing give weight (fewer frames = lighter, more = heavier); a blink uses the muscles around the eye, not only the lids.
- ifra_naaz: floaty animation = missing weight; big spacing = fast, small spacing = slow.
- clubedeanimacao3d: fixing robotic motion (overlap, offsets, arcs).
- dsqracademy: camera moves sorted by feeling: tension = push in; handheld = real; over the shoulder = conversation; POV = be the hero; barrel roll = chaos; bullet time = the impossible moment.
- alittledani: build a facial expression library once (happy, sad, angry, surprised …) and pick from it per shot.
- malbersanimations: animal walk order (hind, front, hind, front).

### Tool landscape seen
cartoon-3d-short / claude-animated-shorts (three.js + Remotion + ffmpeg, MIT), toonwright S2.5 / IMPACT / ROBLOX / build skills, Claude in Blender (elasthic.motion, 3D OS with ~80 agent skills), HyperFrames, Higgsfield (video models), Dreamina, Pollo, Hailuo.

### Patterns across all
- One clear premise in the first second, often a question or a number.
- One world for the whole reel; the camera moves through it (orbit, crane, push-in) instead of cutting between unrelated pictures.
- On-screen counters and timers are very common in 3D hits.
- Few, big, readable actions; many small background motions (busy world).
- Strong two-colour lighting, a bright focus spot, shallow depth of field.
- Episodic hooks: "Part 2", polls, "comment decides".
